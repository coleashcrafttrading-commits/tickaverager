The Finite Ladder

A DCA ladder that trades with the 1-hour trend on either side, enters on the trend's own candle, spaces its rungs by volatility, never holds more than a fifth of the account, and flips the whole position the moment that trend turns.

RAM · 1h trend · flip MSTX · 1h trend · flip shorts: no borrow on either name today cap 20% of equity · 8 rungs

What it does, in one pass

The 1-hour SuperTrend is the trend. Long trend: the first lot opens on the first green one-minute close above the 1-minute EMA20, with a take-profit $0.10 above the fill. Short trend: the first red close below it, sold short, with a buy-back $0.10 below. If price moves one 15-minute ATR against the last fill, add another lot, up to eight rungs or 20% of equity. Each lot leaves through its own take-profit. The moment the 1-hour trend goes the other way, every lot closes at the market and the ladder re-opens on the new side at the same size, lot for lot — the loss is taken; the bet is the other direction.

Why the EMA20 and not VWAP for the entry line: after a flip both fire within a minute, but when the ladder is flat at the open a green close above VWAP waits 16–23 minutes on average and never comes in some sessions, while the EMA20 waits 6–9 and never locks a session out. The 4h EMA50 was ruled out — it would be the regime filter by another name. Existing lots always keep their exits; the trend only ever decides new lots and the flip. The card shows the bar count it is reading from, whether the name can be shorted today, and the exact reason a lot is blocked.

The ladder

The flip

  1. 1
    The 1-hour trend turns against the ladder — at any depth, with no cooldown beyond SuperTrend's own hysteresis (it can turn at most once per closed hour). As soon as the book is a real price (spread no wider than 0.5% of mid — RAM's overnight book has been 5.8% wide), every lot's take-profit is cancelled and one marketable order closes the whole basket through the bid (the ask for a short). If the book moves through it before it prints, it is re-priced and resent, three times at most.
  2. 2
    The moment Alpaca is flat, the ladder re-opens on the new side at the same size: one entry per closed lot, share for share, priced at the quote so it fills now, each with its own take-profit. The 20% cap still binds on the way back in. The re-entry runs outside the 09:35–15:30 new-lot window too — it is the same risk the ladder held a minute earlier — and waits for 04:00 only if the name cannot trade overnight. Alpaca will not cross a position through zero in one order, which is why it is close-then-open; the size is the whole ladder, never one lot.
  3. 3
    If the trend turns back before the re-opening is complete, what is left of the queue is dropped and the lots already re-opened flip again on the next tick — nothing is stranded. A re-entry that dies unfilled goes back to the head of the queue, three tries at most; anything that never re-opens raises a flag that stays on the dashboard. Adds continue afterwards under the normal rules.
One asymmetry to decide: a ladder that is positioned flips at any hour, but a ladder that is flat still waits for the 09:35–15:30 window to open its first lot. 65% of RAM's trend turns and 40% of MSTX's print outside regular hours, most of RAM's into the 05:00 book. The literal reading — enter whenever the 1-hour turns, pre-market included — is a one-line change to session_start / session_end.
Neither RAM nor MSTX can be sold short on this paper account today. Alpaca reports shortable = false and no borrow for both (the engine asks every session and shows the answer on the ticker page). Until a borrow exists, the short leg cannot execute: a long ladder still closes the moment the 1-hour trend turns down, and re-enters long on the first green close above the EMA20 when it turns up again — but nothing is sold short in between, and a short trend at the open is reported as “cannot be sold short at Alpaca today — waiting flat”. Two ways to get the short side: a name Alpaca lends (the engine refuses shorts on one it does not), or the paired inverse ETF as the short leg (MSTZ for MSTX) — the latter is not built.

The flip is what makes two sides survivable: without it a short ladder gets squeezed (MSTX −$14k underwater). With it, on MSTX the live rule beats long-only — +$3,317 against +$2,588 at a smaller worst hole, 2.25 per dollar of drawdown against 1.34: 2,373 closes earned $23.4k of $0.10 wins and 32 flips gave back $20.0k. On RAM it loses — −$597 against +$431: 25 flips cost $15.5k and the ladders earned $14.9k. The trend-coloured entry beat a red-bar entry on MSTX and lost on RAM. The 1-hour trend turns about 2.4–2.5 times a week per name; after a turn the new direction continues 47–58% of the time. Ranked by P/L per dollar of drawdown, never by profit. The replay fills at the touch, knows nothing of halts, partials or borrow, and assumes the short side exists — on this account today it does not. These numbers rank rules. They do not forecast dollars.

More profit, or less?

MSTX: more, with a smaller hole

+$3.3k against +$2.6k long-only, worst open drawdown −$1.5k against −$1.9k. The two-sided ladder closes nine times as many $0.10 lots; the flips eat most of that, not all of it.

RAM: less

−$0.6k against +$0.4k. Same mechanism, thinner name: each flip pays the spread both ways and the ladder's open loss, and RAM's turns cluster in the thinnest book of the day.

Everything above assumes a borrow. The dial for more absolute profit is f_ladder (0.30 gave about 50% more P/L at the same efficiency). A ticker can go back to the researched staged unwind (./apply_v2.sh SYM flatten) or to long-only (side_mode=auto) with one command.
